# MLP and representation study

## Scope and headline

This study asks what the MLPs and residual stream represent in trained tiny transformers for `copy`, `reverse`, `sort`, and `rotate_left`. It uses three independently trained seeds per task, the same 2,048 held-out variable-length sequences, and a common 32-wide, two-layer, four-head, MLP-width-64 architecture. All 12 runs achieved 100% exact-sequence accuracy on this evaluation set.

The strongest reproducible observation is that the first-layer MLP of the routing-dependent tasks (`reverse`, `sort`, `rotate_left`) contains readily linearly decoded position and length information. `copy` does not show the same enrichment. Individual highly selective neurons exist, but ablating the most selective 10% does not consistently hurt more than ablating random neurons. Thus these results support task-dependent, substantially distributed representations; they do **not** identify single causal “algorithm neurons.”

## Method

- Architecture: `d_model=32`, 4 heads, 2 layers, `d_ff=64`, maximum sequence length 9.
- Training: 1,200 steps, batch size 256, AdamW at 0.003; seeds 11, 29, and 47.
- Evaluation: 2,048 fixed held-out sequences, digit lengths 2–8. Padding and the `=` token are excluded from representation analyses.
- Linear probes: ridge least-squares classifiers for input digit, absolute position, and sequence length. The split is by sequence (25% test), preventing tokens from the same sequence appearing in both train and test.
- Selectivity: per-neuron eta-squared, the fraction of activation variance explained by each categorical label. This is descriptive, not causal.
- Intervention: zero the six (10%) MLP neurons with the highest maximum selectivity over digit, position, or length. Compare with 20 random six-neuron ablations per layer and seed.
- Similarity: linear CKA on final-layer residual representations for matched input tokens, computed for corresponding seeds then averaged.

The complete numeric output is in [`research/mlp/results.json`](../research/mlp/results.json), generated by [`research/mlp/analyze.py`](../research/mlp/analyze.py).

## Linear information in MLP activations

Mean held-out probe accuracy across three seeds:

| Task | Layer | Digit | Position | Length |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Copy | 1 | 1.000 | 0.549 | 0.331 |
| Copy | 2 | 1.000 | 0.388 | 0.318 |
| Reverse | 1 | 1.000 | 0.986 | 0.729 |
| Reverse | 2 | 0.999 | 0.758 | 0.472 |
| Sort | 1 | 1.000 | 0.733 | 0.397 |
| Sort | 2 | 1.000 | 0.467 | 0.314 |
| Rotate left | 1 | 1.000 | 0.979 | 0.642 |
| Rotate left | 2 | 1.000 | 0.800 | 0.391 |

The majority baselines vary slightly by split; they are approximately 0.10 for digit, 0.16 for position, and 0.14–0.15 for length. Digit identity remains perfectly decodable, which is expected because token information persists through residual connections. More interestingly, reverse and rotation make absolute position almost perfectly recoverable in layer 1, with sequence length also substantially recoverable. Both are needed to map an input position to a length-dependent destination. Sort has weaker positional decoding, consistent with its output depending more on multiset/rank than source position. This is an architectural correlation, not proof that the probes recover the variables in the form used by the computation.

Probe accuracy generally falls in layer 2. One plausible interpretation is that layer 1 constructs routing features and layer 2 consumes or mixes them; probing alone cannot establish that direction of causality.

## Neuron selectivity

Mean maximum eta-squared over neurons:

| Task | Layer | Digit | Position | Length |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Copy | 1 | 0.999 | 0.002 | 0.003 |
| Copy | 2 | 0.999 | 0.002 | 0.002 |
| Reverse | 1 | 0.963 | 0.727 | 0.237 |
| Reverse | 2 | 0.565 | 0.245 | 0.094 |
| Sort | 1 | 0.980 | 0.467 | 0.077 |
| Sort | 2 | 0.774 | 0.398 | 0.040 |
| Rotate left | 1 | 0.985 | 0.772 | 0.150 |
| Rotate left | 2 | 0.521 | 0.253 | 0.038 |

Copy MLP neurons are extremely digit-selective and essentially not position- or length-selective. Reverse and rotate-left develop strongly position-selective first-layer neurons, while sort is intermediate. Length selectivity is weaker and less localized than position selectivity. The decline in maximum digit selectivity in layer 2 for the routing tasks suggests more mixed features, whereas copy preserves nearly pure digit tuning throughout.

Eta-squared can be inflated by correlated labels and only detects categorical mean differences. In particular, position and length are statistically dependent in padded variable-length data, so their selectivity values should not be interpreted as cleanly disentangled factors.

## Causal ablations

Mean exact-sequence accuracy after ablating 10% of each MLP:

| Task | Layer | Most selective | Random mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Copy | 1 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Copy | 2 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Reverse | 1 | 0.999 | 0.942 |
| Reverse | 2 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Sort | 1 | 0.966 | 0.955 |
| Sort | 2 | 1.000 | 0.995 |
| Rotate left | 1 | 0.979 | 0.940 |
| Rotate left | 2 | 1.000 | 1.000 |

The selected neurons are not consistently more causally important than random sets. For reverse and rotate-left, random layer-1 ablations are actually more damaging on average. This can happen because selectivity is not importance, the maximum-over-labels rule heavily favors digit-selective units, and weight/output magnitude is ignored. The main defensible conclusion is that performance is robust to removing the small selected sets and the computation is not localized by this criterion. A stronger follow-up would compare activation patching, mean rather than zero ablation, and output-weight-adjusted attribution.

## Representational similarity across tasks

Mean final-residual linear CKA for matched inputs was low across different tasks: 0.046–0.114. Reverse and rotate-left were most similar (0.114), while copy and rotate-left were least similar (0.046). This is compatible with task-specific representational geometries and a modest commonality between two positional routing operations.

However, these are independently initialized models, CKA is sensitive to dataset composition and nonlinear reparameterizations, and the range is small. The ordering is descriptive rather than a robust taxonomy; confidence intervals over more seeds would be needed for a strong cross-task claim.

## Reproduction

From the repository root:

```bash
python research/mlp/analyze.py --steps 1200 --seeds 11 29 47 --eval 2048
```

The script selects CUDA when available and overwrites `research/mlp/results.json`. On the RTX 4080 used here, the full run took roughly two minutes while another research job also shared the GPU.

## Limitations and next experiments

- Only one architecture and one training hyperparameter setting were studied. Minimal preset models may organize representations differently.
- Evaluation is randomly generated, not exhaustive over the enormous input space, despite perfect measured accuracy.
- Probe success establishes accessible information, not use by the model.
- Zero ablation is potentially off-distribution. Mean ablation and activation patching are better controls.
- Labels are correlated, especially position and length.
- Three seeds are enough to reject anecdotes but not to tightly estimate effect sizes.

High-value next steps are path patching from source token to destination token, causal scrubbing against explicit routing hypotheses, sparse dictionary learning on combined MLP activations, and comparing full-width models with the Pareto-minimal presets.
