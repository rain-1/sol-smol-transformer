# Attention circuits in the tiny operation transformers

## Summary

Across 1,792 fresh evaluation sequences per task (256 at each digit length 2–8), the
two-layer reverse model learned the clearest algorithmic circuit: both layer-2
heads route every output query to the position reflected across the sequence
midpoint. Their mean attention mass on the required source is 0.964–0.965 and
their source is top-ranked on 100% of digit queries. The one-layer rotate model
learned the analogous shift-by-one routing circuit (0.723 mean required-source
mass; 82.7% top-1). Copy and sort use qualitatively different solutions rather
than smaller versions of the reverse circuit.

All numbers below come from `research/attention/results.json`, generated by
`research/attention/analyze.py`. The script trains the published preset-sized
architectures for 2,000 steps at seed 42 and saves the exact checkpoints used.

## Experimental design

The evaluation is exhaustive over supported *lengths*, not over digit strings.
For each length 2–8, it samples 256 independently generated strings and captures
attention for every layer and head. Digit-position queries are scored against:

- the required source position for copy, reverse, and rotate;
- diagonal and anti-diagonal mass;
- mass assigned to the terminal `=` key;
- entropy normalized by the maximum entropy at that length;
- standard deviation across examples at fixed query/key coordinates, a coarse
  measure of content dependence.

For causal evidence, every head is independently removed by zeroing its slice of
the attention output projection, then exact-sequence accuracy is measured on a
fixed 3,584-example set. This preserves all other learned weights. Baselines were
100% except sort (99.50%).

## Reverse: a two-stage positional circuit

Layer 1 does not reverse. H0 sends 59.9% of its mass to `=` and H1 is diffuse
(normalized entropy 0.917). Layer 2 changes abruptly: both heads become nearly
identical low-entropy anti-diagonal routers.

| Head | Required-source mass | Required source top-1 | Entropy | `=` mass | Accuracy after ablation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| L0H0 | .100 | 4.3% | .645 | .599 | 36.08% |
| L0H1 | .173 | 16.1% | .917 | .300 | 6.31% |
| L1H0 | .964 | 100% | .065 | .000 | 28.96% |
| L1H1 | .965 | 100% | .066 | .000 | 32.67% |

The layer-2 rule generalizes across the trained variable lengths. Required-source
mass for L1H0 is 1.00 at length 2, .99 at 4, .97 at 6, and .87 at 8; top-1 stays
100%. Low across-example variation (.006–.007) shows routing is positional rather
than determined by digit identity. A plausible circuit is therefore: layer 1
constructs/broadcasts length or boundary information from `=`, while layer 2 uses
that contextualized representation to calculate `length - 1 - query_position`
and retrieves the digit. Layer-1 ablations being severe supports necessity, but
the current measurements do **not** identify what representation it writes.

The two final heads are redundant in pattern but not function: ablating either
loses 67–71 percentage points of exact accuracy. They may transmit complementary
value subspaces while sharing a routing pattern; attention maps alone cannot show
this.

## Rotate left: direct offset routing

The single head places .723 mean mass on `(query + 1) mod digit_length`, selects
that position top-1 on 82.7% of queries, and has low content variation (.017).
Its removal collapses exact accuracy from 100% to 0.11%, strong evidence that the
head implements the operation rather than merely correlating with it. Performance
is perfect even where routing is not top-1, because weighted value mixtures and
the residual/MLP can still decode the correct digit. The wraparound query is the
main special case and likely accounts for some less-sharp averages.

## Copy: local residual plus a content/boundary head

Copy's head is not a pure identity router: diagonal mass is .454, diagonal top-1
is 51.4%, `=` receives .369, and across-example variation is unusually high
(.228). Copy has an architectural shortcut—the input digit embedding is already
in the residual stream at the output position—so attention need not transport the
token. Nevertheless, ablating the head lowers exact accuracy to 34.15%. The head
likely supplies boundary/position and content-conditioned corrections that make
the residual digit linearly decodable. This is a useful warning against naming a
head from the task alone: the “copy head” is neither consistently diagonal nor
sufficiently understood by its attention pattern.

## Sort: global content aggregation, not positional routing

Sort has no fixed source position because the source of output rank *q* depends on
all digit values. Its only head is broad (normalized entropy .902), mildly
content-dependent (.067), and attends to `=` with .272 mean mass. Ablation reduces
99.50% exact accuracy to 0.45%, making it the most clearly indispensable head
alongside rotate. The broad pattern is consistent with collecting a global digit
histogram or rank evidence into every position; it is not proof of that mechanism.
Value-vector and MLP analyses are needed to test whether digit counts or threshold
features are represented.

## What the `=` token does

The terminal token is not just an output delimiter. In reverse layer 1 it is the
dominant key (.599/.300 by head) but disappears from layer-2 routing. That is the
strongest evidence that `=` acts as a variable-length boundary signal. Copy and
sort also allocate substantial mass to it (.369 and .272). Rotate uses less on
average (.157), though the value changes by length. Because padding was absent in
these fixed-length evaluation batches, these effects cannot be attributed to pad
masking.

## Limitations and next tests

This is one seed and one checkpoint per task; circuits may not be unique. Training
lengths are 2–8, so results show interpolation among trained lengths, not
out-of-distribution length generalization. Attention weights are descriptive and
can obscure value-space computation. Output-projection ablation is causal but may
push activations off distribution, and exact-sequence accuracy amplifies small
token error rates.

High-value follow-ups are: repeat across seeds and align heads by behavior; patch
the `=` residual stream between lengths; decompose head outputs by value direction;
probe reverse layer 1 for encoded length; and test sort activations for digit-count
and cumulative-count features. The current positional embeddings cap evaluation
at length 9, so true extrapolation requires extending or replacing them.

## Reproduction

```bash
python research/attention/analyze.py --steps 2000 --examples 256 --seed 42
```

Artifacts are in `research/attention/results.json` and
`research/attention/checkpoints/`. Exact values, including per-length results, are
retained in JSON rather than rounded report tables.
