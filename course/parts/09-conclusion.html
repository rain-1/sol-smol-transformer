Four ideas worth keeping

1. Architecture decides which algorithms are reachable.

The same task was unlearnable for the encoder and easy for the causal decoder, because value-routing is naturally an induction copy — a circuit that forms readily when generation is left-to-right. When a model can't learn something, the representation it would need may simply not be reachable by its architecture.

2. Models take the easiest path; design the task to force the mechanism.

Plain sorting became counting, not comparison, because counting was easier. If you want to study a particular circuit, you have to build a task where nothing simpler will do. Half of interpretability is choosing the right model organism.

3. Geometry follows computation.

Keys got an ordered embedding because they are compared; values got an arbitrary one because they are only copied. The clean circles and helixes you see in famous results appear precisely when the task forces that structure. Shape is a shadow of function.

4. Observation proposes; intervention disposes.

Every real claim here survived an experiment that could have refuted it. And every mistake we made was the same shape: reading a coarse signal (necessary, decodable) as a precise one (does X, is used, is localized). Hold observations as hypotheses until a sharp test promotes them.

A trained network is not magic and it is not a lookup table. It is a specific algorithm, discovered by gradient descent, that you can read out if you are willing to look and then test what you see.

Try it yourself

Everything in this course is reproducible. The model, the training, and every analysis live in research/sort_scaling/, and the write-ups with full numbers are in reports/. Concrete next steps, in rough order of difficulty:

  • Run research/sort_scaling/walkthrough.py and change the example input; watch the induction head re-target.
  • Ablate a different head in deep_interp.py and predict the effect before you look.
  • Train a fresh seed and find the induction head yourself — by role, not by index.
  • Design a new task that would force a different circuit (say, keys you must add rather than compare) and see what geometry appears.

Where this fits

The techniques you practised — reading attention, ablation and patching, probing, and geometric analysis — are the same ones used on frontier models with billions of parameters. The difference is only that here you could hold the entire circuit in your head at once. That is the point of a model organism: learn the moves where you can check your work, then take them somewhere you cannot.
